The states that decide whether you trust it

A digest that only looks good on a perfect day isn't designed yet. These are the two runs that determine whether this stays in your inbox or gets muted.

Quiet day No email sent

Nothing scored above threshold, so nothing arrives. A daily "0 new roles today" message would train you to ignore the sender within a week — silence is the design, not a gap in it.

Inbox unchanged. Run logged, state saved.

Why each element is there

Nothing in the row is decorative — if an element doesn't change your open-or-skip decision, it shouldn't survive to v1.

  • Score, not a rank An absolute 0–100 lets you calibrate your threshold over time. A ranked list would hide that today's best role is mediocre.
  • One-sentence reason The single highest-value field. It's what lets you skip a role confidently without opening it — which is the entire time saving.
  • Concerns surfaced, not buried Amber tags name the dealbreaker up front. A digest that only shows upside becomes a digest you stop believing.
  • Source shown Knowing a role came from Greenhouse versus HTML scraping tells you how much to trust it, and which adapter to blame when something looks wrong.
  • Counts in the footer "34 checked, 11 new, 8 filtered" is the proof the system ran properly. Without it, a quiet day and a broken crawl look identical.
  • Link to your own config Every digest ends by reminding you the filter is yours to tune. Week-one quality comes from editing that file, not from changing code.
